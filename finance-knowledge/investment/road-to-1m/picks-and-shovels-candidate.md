**Verdict:** one of the best candidates for your thesis.

### 2. **Custom AI silicon + optical/networking ASICs: Marvell**

**Ticker: MRVL**

Broadcom is the obvious winner in custom AI ASICs, especially with Google TPU exposure. Broadcom reported Q1 AI revenue of **$8.4B**, up **106% YoY**, driven by custom AI accelerators and AI networking, and guided Q2 AI semiconductor revenue to **$10.7B**.

But that is the problem: Broadcom is already a **$2T+ market cap** company. The thesis is increasingly consensus.

Marvell is the more interesting second-order play. It has exposure to custom silicon, DSPs, optical interconnect, and data center networking. It is smaller, more volatile, and less de-risked than Broadcom, but that is exactly why it may still have more mispricing potential. Marvell recently delivered record quarterly revenue of about **$2.2B** in fiscal Q4 2026.

Why the moat lasts ~3 years: hyperscaler custom ASIC programs are long design cycles. Once Marvell is designed into a platform, the revenue can persist across multiple product generations.

Why it may not be fully priced: MRVL is expensive on headline P/E, around **58×**, but far less mega-cap crowded than AVGO/NVDA. It is more of an “earnings inflection” bet than a cheap stock.

**Verdict:** higher-risk, higher-upside version of the Broadcom custom-silicon thesis.

### 3. **Optical interconnect / transceivers: Coherent, Fabrinet, maybe MaxLinear**

**Tickers: COHR, FN, MXL**

AI clusters are becoming networking machines. As cluster size scales, the spend shifts from “just GPUs” to **GPU + HBM + networking + optics + switches + cables + power + cooling**. Optical links become increasingly important as clusters move to 800G, 1.6T, and eventually higher.

Coherent is interesting because it sells lasers, optical components, transceivers, and indium phosphide technology. Recent commentary says AI datacenter demand is lifting revenue, bookings visibility, 800G/1.6T mix, optical circuit switches, and co-packaged optics traction.

Fabrinet is the “manufacturing picks-and-shovels” version: it builds advanced optical communications products for customers. Its optical communications revenue reportedly hit about **$833M** in fiscal Q2 2026, lifting total sales to a record **$1.13B**.

Why the moat lasts ~3 years: optical manufacturing at scale is hard, customer qualification matters, and data center optical roadmaps are moving faster than many incumbents can support.

Why it may not be fully priced: these names are now discovered, but they are still much smaller than the GPU/ASIC leaders. Coherent is about **$63B market cap**, Fabrinet about **$26B**, versus Nvidia at ~$5.1T and Broadcom at ~$2.05T.

Key risk: optical names can be brutally cyclical and customer-concentrated. Also, COHR’s headline P/E is very high, so you need to underwrite forward earnings normalization, not trailing earnings.

**Verdict:** good “less obvious than GPUs” basket; I would prefer basket exposure over a single name.

### 4. **Electrical equipment / power distribution: Eaton, GE Vernova, Vertiv**

**Most balanced: ETN. Most direct but priced: VRT, GEV.**

This may be the most underappreciated structural bottleneck by software investors: **data centers need power before they need GPUs**. Transformers, switchgear, UPS systems, thermal management, turbines, grid equipment, and medium-voltage gear are becoming gating constraints.

GE Vernova just raised 2026 guidance, citing data center and AI-driven power-equipment demand. Backlog rose to about **$163B**, and management expects backlog to reach **$200B by 2027**.

Vertiv is very direct to AI data center cooling and power. It reported Q1 2026 sales of **$2.65B**, up **30% YoY**, and raised full-year guidance.

But both VRT and GEV have been strongly re-rated. Vertiv trades around **81× trailing earnings**, and GE Vernova is already around **$313B market cap** after a major rally.

Eaton is less pure-play but potentially more balanced. It has electrical infrastructure exposure, data center power exposure, industrial diversification, and less single-theme fragility. Eaton trades around **42× trailing earnings**, which is not cheap, but less extreme than Vertiv.

**Verdict:** I like ETN as the lower-beta “AI power grid” compounder; VRT/GEV are excellent businesses but more likely already reflect the AI infrastructure story.

### 5. **Advanced packaging / semiconductor equipment: Lam, Amkor, ASML, TSMC**

This is a critical bottleneck, but valuation and geography matter.

TSMC is the most important physical manufacturing bottleneck in AI. But it is too obvious, enormous, geopolitically exposed, and already central to every AI thesis. ASML is also a monopoly-like seller, but EUV demand is broadly understood.

The more interesting sub-bucket is **advanced packaging**: CoWoS-like capacity, HBM integration, interposers, substrates, and test/assembly. Amkor is one public name to watch, but its moat is weaker than TSMC’s and its AI exposure is less clean. Lam also benefits from memory and advanced process equipment; Lam recently beat earnings expectations, with management citing AI-driven demand and capex from TSMC and Micron.

**Verdict:** strategically important, but I would not make this my first basket unless you want a semiconductor capex-cycle trade.
