# Leveraged Index ETF

Leveraged ETFs are exchange-traded funds that allow investors to amplify their returns through the use of financial leverage. These funds typically use derivatives, such as options and futures contracts, to achieve a leverage ratio of two or three times the daily performance of a specific index or benchmark.

# Leveraged ETF Decay

meaning the returns can diverge significantly from the underlying index.

Caused by compounding effect of daily returns “volatility drag”

# Beta in Leveraged Index ETF

So I think leveraged index ETF would be a good choice in only certain cases

It is true that all ETF tracing a certain index will experience decay (leveraged ETF might be more so.

So the total expected return of the leverage index ETF will be 

$$
{Expected Return}_{leveraged} = {Expected Return}_{underlining index} - Decay
$$

However, the beta of the Leveraged ETF will be multiple of the underlining ETF, and since the ETF itself is diversified, all the unsystematic risk are diversified away. 

In the long run, betting in leveraged index ETF will be a negative EV compare to betting in the unleveraged index ETF, however, if you can somehow be somewhat sure of the general trend of the market, meaning you could somewhat time the bottom of the market, leveraged index ETF might be a good bet. next you need to figure out how much of this certainty you need to have to balance out the lost from decay and increased beta.
